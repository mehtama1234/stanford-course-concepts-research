Policy

The rule an agent uses to choose what to do next.

What real-world problem is this about?

The problem is that the output is an action, and that action changes what happens afterward.

Why does this problem exist?

In a changing environment, the learner must connect what it can see now to a choice under uncertainty.

What is the mathematical idea underneath?

A policy is often written as pi(a|s): the chance of taking action a when the system is in situation s.

Why is this concept important?

It is the central object being learned in reinforcement learning and agentic LLM systems.

What breaks without it?

There is no explicit way to discuss or improve the system's behavior over time.

Naive Starting Point

Start with the simplest version: The problem is that the output is an action, and that action changes what happens afterward. A beginner might try to solve this with a fixed rule, a lookup table, or one direct guess. That works only while the situation is tiny and clean.

Why the Simple Approach Fails

The simple approach breaks because in a changing environment, the learner must connect what it can see now to a choice under uncertainty. Once the input grows, feedback arrives late, or many explanations compete, the system needs a reusable mechanism instead of a special case.

The Mathematical Object

The useful object is the thing the model can change or compare while learning Policy: A policy is often written as pi(a|s): the chance of taking action a when the system is in situation s. Think of it as the handle the algorithm can grab.

The Operation

The operation is to apply that object repeatedly: read the current situation, produce a numerical signal, and use feedback or comparison to adjust the next step. For Policy, this is the move that turns a vague goal into an executable procedure.

Worked Mini-Example

Mini-example: a robot sees a block and must decide whether to push, lift, wait, or move its camera. A classifier answer is not enough because the output changes the next situation. In CS224R, policies appear as the behavior rule that maps state or task information to actions. The hierarchical RL lecture makes this concrete by asking how a policy can do multiple tasks, adapt from examples, and string shorter behaviors into a longer-horizon task.

What the Lectures Emphasize

CS224R emphasizes policy as the central object of behavior. A policy can be conditioned on a task descriptor, a goal state, or experience from a new task; it can also be organized hierarchically when a long task requires multiple skills. The lecture sequence makes clear that a policy is not just a prediction label. It is the rule that changes what data the agent will see next.

Common Misunderstanding

A common misunderstanding is that a policy is always a fixed plan. In modern RL it is often a learned conditional distribution over actions. It can be stochastic, task-conditioned, hierarchical, or adapted after seeing a few examples.

How to Recognize This in a New Paper or Model

In a new paper, recognize policy whenever the system chooses actions from observations, prompts, states, goals, or histories. The method may call it an actor, controller, planner, decoder, behavior policy, or action model. The shared primitive is choice under consequence: the output becomes part of the future.

Connected Concepts

The same primitive reappears near reward, policy gradient, q learning. Across the three courses, the shared pattern is search, uncertainty: choose what to keep, how to move, what feedback means, or how to measure whether the behavior really improved.

Lecture Argument
The CS224R Q-learning lecture distinguishes value functions and Q-functions by saying a value function follows a policy from a state, while a Q-function starts with a state-action choice and then follows a policy.
Example Or Analogy
The policy is the rule for what happens after the current moment. A value estimate asks, 'if I keep following this behavior rule, what future reward should I expect?'
Mathematical Claim
A policy maps states to actions or action probabilities. Value functions are defined relative to that policy, so the behavior rule determines which future trajectory is being evaluated.
Caveat Or Warning
This span supports policy as the behavior being followed, not a complete treatment of how policies are learned or represented in every algorithm.
Why This Span Matters
It anchors policy as the object that gives value estimates their meaning in RL.
