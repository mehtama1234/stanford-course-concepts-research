Actor-Critic

A learner split into a chooser and a judge.

What real-world problem is this about?

The problem is that raw rewards are noisy, so the agent needs a better estimate of whether an action was actually good.

Why does this problem exist?

Decision making improves when action choice and outcome evaluation can help each other.

What is the mathematical idea underneath?

The actor updates the policy; the critic estimates value. The actor asks what to do, and the critic estimates how good the situation or action is.

Why is this concept important?

It reduces noise in learning and underlies many practical RL methods.

What breaks without it?

Policy updates can be too unstable or data-hungry for complex environments.

Naive Starting Point

Start with the simplest version: The problem is that raw rewards are noisy, so the agent needs a better estimate of whether an action was actually good. A beginner might try to solve this with a fixed rule, a lookup table, or one direct guess. That works only while the situation is tiny and clean.

Why the Simple Approach Fails

The simple approach breaks because decision making improves when action choice and outcome evaluation can help each other. Once the input grows, feedback arrives late, or many explanations compete, the system needs a reusable mechanism instead of a special case.

The Mathematical Object

The useful object is the thing the model can change or compare while learning Actor-Critic: The actor updates the policy; the critic estimates value. The actor asks what to do, and the critic estimates how good the situation or action is. Think of it as the handle the algorithm can grab.

The Operation

The operation is to apply that object repeatedly: read the current situation, produce a numerical signal, and use feedback or comparison to adjust the next step. For Actor-Critic, this is the move that turns a vague goal into an executable procedure.

Worked Mini-Example

Mini-example: suppose an agent takes an action and receives a noisy final score. The actor is the part that chooses actions. The critic is the part that estimates whether the situation or action was better than expected. In the CS224R recap, policy gradients improve a policy from collected data, baselines encourage more above-average behavior, and value functions estimate future expected rewards. Actor-critic methods combine those roles: the actor changes behavior, while the critic supplies a lower-noise judgment signal.

What the Lectures Emphasize

CS224R emphasizes actor-critic as a practical response to noisy policy-gradient learning. The course moves from policy-gradient updates, to baselines, to value functions and Q-functions, then into off-policy actor-critic. That sequence matters: the critic is not an ornament. It is introduced because raw trajectory returns are too noisy and because multiple gradient steps or off-policy data require more careful correction.

Common Misunderstanding

A common misunderstanding is that the critic is a separate judge with ground truth. It is also learned and can be wrong. If the critic overestimates unsupported actions or gives biased value estimates, the actor can confidently move in the wrong direction.

How to Recognize This in a New Paper or Model

In a new paper, recognize actor-critic structure whenever one learned object chooses actions and another estimates value, advantage, reward-to-go, or quality. It may appear in robotics, PPO-style LLM tuning, off-policy RL, or continuous-control methods. The shared primitive is division of labor: separate choosing from judging so feedback is less noisy.

Connected Concepts

The same primitive reappears near policy gradient, value functions, rl for llms. Across the three courses, the shared pattern is feedback, credit: choose what to keep, how to move, what feedback means, or how to measure whether the behavior really improved.

Example Or Analogy
The baseline is like grading relative to the usual outcome. An action is not reinforced just because it got some reward; it is reinforced when it does better than the comparison point.
Mathematical Claim
A baseline reduces variance in policy-gradient updates by subtracting a reference value from returns. Actor-critic methods learn a value-like critic to provide a better comparison signal.
Caveat Or Warning
A baseline can reduce noise, but a bad critic can bias or destabilize learning. The span is a bridge into actor-critic, not a full guarantee.
