Diffusion

Generating by starting from noise and repeatedly making it more like data.

What real-world problem is this about?

The problem is that realistic images or videos are too complex to create in one jump from a prompt.

Why does this problem exist?

A hard generation problem can be broken into many easier cleanup steps.

What is the mathematical idea underneath?

A forward process adds noise; a learned reverse process removes it. Each step estimates how to move a noisy sample toward the data distribution.

Why is this concept important?

It is a major recipe for high-quality image, video, and multimodal generation.

What breaks without it?

Generation must rely on harder one-shot mappings or unstable adversarial training.

Naive Starting Point

Start with the simplest version: The problem is that realistic images or videos are too complex to create in one jump from a prompt. A beginner might try to solve this with a fixed rule, a lookup table, or one direct guess. That works only while the situation is tiny and clean.

Why the Simple Approach Fails

The simple approach breaks because a hard generation problem can be broken into many easier cleanup steps. Once the input grows, feedback arrives late, or many explanations compete, the system needs a reusable mechanism instead of a special case.

The Mathematical Object

The useful object is the thing the model can change or compare while learning Diffusion: A forward process adds noise; a learned reverse process removes it. Each step estimates how to move a noisy sample toward the data distribution. Think of it as the handle the algorithm can grab.

The Operation

The operation is to apply that object repeatedly: read the current situation, produce a numerical signal, and use feedback or comparison to adjust the next step. For Diffusion, this is the move that turns a vague goal into an executable procedure.

Worked Mini-Example

Mini-example: start with a photo and gradually corrupt it until only static remains. That forward process is easy: keep adding noise. The hard question is how to reverse it. Diffusion training gives the model noisy examples and teaches it the direction back toward cleaner, more data-like samples. At generation time the model starts from random noise and repeatedly applies those learned cleanup steps. The CME296 lecture sequence frames this as learning to generate from an unknown and complicated data distribution, beginning with DDPM-style diffusion and then comparing it to score matching.

What the Lectures Emphasize

The CME296 lectures emphasize diffusion as a generation paradigm: the goal is to create new samples from a complicated data distribution that is not directly known. Lecture 1 introduces the DDPM route through clean data, noise, and a reverse process. Lecture 2 explicitly compares that route to score matching, treating both as ways to get a direction for moving samples toward the data. Later lectures connect this to guidance, latent spaces, architecture, training, and evaluation.

Common Misunderstanding

A common misunderstanding is that diffusion models store training images and slowly reveal them. The first-principles picture is different: the model learns local directions for making a noisy sample more likely under the data. The generated image is produced by a path through uncertainty, not by retrieving a memorized clean image.

How to Recognize This in a New Paper or Model

In a new paper, recognize diffusion when generation is described as a sequence of small updates from a simple noisy starting point toward structured data. The paper may use noise schedules, denoisers, scores, samplers, reverse processes, latent diffusion, rectified flows, or guidance. The shared primitive is guided movement: replace one impossible jump with many learnable steps.

Connected Concepts

The same primitive reappears near score matching, guidance, latent space. Across the three courses, the shared pattern is uncertainty, search: choose what to keep, how to move, what feedback means, or how to measure whether the behavior really improved.
