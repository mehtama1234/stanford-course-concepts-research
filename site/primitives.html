Mathematical Primitives

The same small patterns recur across transformers, RL, diffusion, and vision.

Compression

Keep the parts that matter for the next decision and let go of the rest.

Why it exists: Inputs, memories, images, and action histories are too large to carry forward unchanged.

Everyday Setup
Compression starts from an ordinary constraint: Inputs, memories, images, and action histories are too large to carry forward unchanged. In the course atlas it appears in tokenization, embeddings, attention, latent space, where the same pressure shows up with different surface names.
Formal Object
The formal object is the reusable handle that lets a model act on this pressure. For compression, that handle may be a vector, score, value estimate, probability, update rule, or state representation depending on the course.
Useful Equation
compressed = keep(signal) - discard(noise)
Symbol Explanation
`signal` means useful structure, `noise` means detail that does not help the next task, and `keep`/`discard` describe the representational choice.
Where It Appears
In the transformer course, the primitive usually appears as a way to represent, route, or update context. In the RL course, it appears as a way to choose actions under delayed feedback. In the diffusion and vision course, it appears as a way to move through high-dimensional sample spaces.
Misuse Failure
When compression is misused, the system optimizes the wrong handle: it may throw away needed detail, assign responsibility to the wrong source, trust unsupported futures, or report a score that hides the actual failure.

Concepts: tokenization, embeddings, attention, latent space
